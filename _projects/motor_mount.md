### Summary

This project focused on the design and fabrication of a custom motor mount to securely support and align propulsion motors with our aircraft. The mount was needed because the motors we selected that year did not come with cross mounts like they usually do, which left us with no way to attach the motor to the airframe. Designing and making a custom mount became critical to the plane’s progress, especially since an iteration of the aircraft was due the week after the motor arrived. It was a time-sensitive problem, and one that absolutely had to be solved.


I designed the motor mount from scratch in CAD, iterating on the geometry, mounting interfaces, and tolerances to make sure the motor would align properly and integrate with the rest of the structure. At the start of this project, no one on our team—including myself—had any prior experience with machining or design for manufacturing (DFM), so this was a big learning curve. I took the lead on figuring it out, turning the CAD model into a manufacturable part and fabricating it myself using a manual milling machine. This involved material selection, setup planning, fixturing, and a lot of careful measuring to hit the required dimensions.


The process involved a lot of trying, failing, and trying again. The first iteration was sketched by hand and dimensioned using calipers, while later versions were fully modeled in Fusion 360 with proper engineering drawings and dimensions pulled from the motor supplier’s specs. Through multiple iterations, I learned a lot about careful design, why tolerancing matters so much in DFM, and how designs change once you actually start cutting material. Three iterations later, I'm very well acquainted with the milling machine!
